The string operators allow us to understand the profile of fractional excitations in the z z direction, which is determined by the fractional part of K − 1 K^{-1} (the integral part of K − 1 K^{-1} corresponds to local fermion and integer flux excitations). In particular, for the example of K nF K_{\text{nF}} , the entries of each row of K nF − 1 K_{\text{nF}}^{-1} decay exponentially, which means that both 𝒲 2 i \mathcal{W}^{i}_{2} and 𝒲 3 i \mathcal{W}^{i}_{3} become exponentially close to the identity as we move in the z z direction ( 𝒲 1 i \mathcal{W}^{i}_{1} is always local in the z z direction). Therefore, 𝒲 i \mathcal{W}^{i} is local in the z z direction with an exponentially decaying tail, and the fractional excitations are localized particles. On the other hand, for K gl K_{\text{gl}} , the fractional parts of the entries of K gl − 1 K_{\text{gl}}^{-1} do not decay. This means that the fractional excitations in the K gl K_{\text{gl}} theory, if valid at all, are line excitations extended along the z z direction.
